## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This was the second day of Portland's FY2026-27 budget deliberations, focused primarily on amendments to restore public safety funding cuts proposed in Mayor Keith Wilson's budget. Jamie Dunphy (District 1, Council President) led the session through a structured process of considering four major amendment packages designed to backfill approximately $10+ million in cuts to police, fire, and related services.

The session was dominated by heated debate over two competing philosophies: using underspent funds from the newly-created Community Board for Police Accountability (CBPA) versus making cuts elsewhere in city operations. Angelita Morillo (District 3) successfully passed her amendment [01:11:57] cutting council security funding by $2.5 million to restore ceasefire programs, summer youth programming, CHAT services, and Portland Street Response with an 8-4 vote. However, a larger $10 million proposal from Olivia Clark (District 4) and co-sponsors to borrow from CBPA funds faced fierce opposition, with Sameer Kanal (District 2) and Candace Avalos (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) arguing it violated the voter-approved charter mandate requiring 5% of police budget for accountability functions.

The meeting revealed deep philosophical divisions about fiscal priorities, with councilors like Dan Ryan (District 2) and Loretta Smith (District 1) emphasizing the need to restore frontline services in underserved East Portland, while others like Tiffany Koyama Lane (District 3) and Mitch Green (District 4) pushed for administrative cuts instead. Security concerns were also prominent, with detailed discussion of threats faced by councilors and the need for protective services.
