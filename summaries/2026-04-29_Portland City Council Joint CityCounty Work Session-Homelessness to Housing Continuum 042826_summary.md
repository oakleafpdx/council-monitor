## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This joint work session between Portland City Council and Multnomah County focused on the homelessness to housing continuum, featuring budget presentations from both jurisdictions and data analysis of system performance. The session was structured around three main components: current homelessness data trends, proposed executive budgets from Mayor Keith Wilson and Chair Vega-Peterson, and projected impacts of various investment scenarios.

The data presentation revealed concerning trends, with inflow into homelessness consistently outpacing outflow by approximately 360 people per month over the last six months. The by-name list of people experiencing homelessness has grown to 18,250 people as of February 2026 [00:05:52]. Both jurisdictions face significant budget constraints, with the county proposing a $67 million gap in homeless services and the city planning to end its $31 million pass-through funding to the county in favor of direct service provision.

A major theme throughout the discussion was the tension between maintaining shelter capacity versus investing in prevention and housing placement services. County staff demonstrated through modeling that rebalancing investments toward prevention and rapid rehousing could "bend the curve" of homelessness growth, even if it doesn't achieve immediate reductions. The presentations suggested that for every $5 million invested in housing placements, approximately 300 people could be moved into permanent housing [00:40:19].

The session revealed significant coordination challenges between city and county systems, with several councilors and commissioners expressing frustration about duplicative services, parallel administrative structures, and the need for clearer role definition. Particular attention was paid to the 1,600 vacant affordable housing units and concerns about Home Forward's performance in managing public housing assets.
