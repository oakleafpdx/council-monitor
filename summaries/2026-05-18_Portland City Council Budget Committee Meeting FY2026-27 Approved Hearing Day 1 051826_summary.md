## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Portland City Council convened as the Budget Committee for Day 1 of hearings on the FY2026-27 approved budget, with Council President Jamie Dunphy (District 1) presiding [00:05:01]. The meeting began with procedural items including a state-required hearing on revenue sharing and consideration of three technical budget amendments. A contentious exchange occurred early in the meeting when Loretta Smith (District 1) challenged Dunphy regarding a housing ordinance that had been indefinitely postponed, which Dunphy ruled out of order [00:11:22].

The bulk of the meeting consisted of public testimony from nearly 150 speakers, each given 90 seconds to address the budget and proposed amendments. Major themes included opposition to public safety cuts (particularly Engine 22 closure in St. Johns), concerns about core services realignment affecting city workers, and debates over Portland Clean Energy Fund (PCF) allocations. City employees dominated testimony, with many sharing layoff notices received as part of the mayor's core services realignment initiative.

Three technical amendments were approved to correct budget errors, though Councilor Eric Zimmerman (District 4) expressed concern about the unusual number and substance of technical corrections [00:28:02]. Council President Dunphy recused himself from Technical Amendment 3 due to his spouse's employment at Leach Botanical Gardens [00:20:37]. The meeting established the framework for subsequent amendment discussions, with Dunphy noting amendments fell into five thematic areas: public safety, PCF dollars, PMO and trees, parks cuts, and citywide governance.

Public testimony revealed significant opposition to proposed cuts across multiple bureaus, with particular emphasis on maintaining fire and police services, preserving tree canopy programs funded by PCF, and halting what many characterized as a rushed core services realignment process. The testimony phase concluded after several hours, setting up Tuesday and Wednesday sessions for amendment consideration and voting.

## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### City Budget & Finance
The meeting centered on a budget crisis requiring significant cuts across city services. Budget Director Ruth Levine (City Budget Director) explained technical amendments totaling over $35 million in appropriation changes, though much represented accounting adjustments rather than new spending [00:15:44]. The approved budget process will continue through June, with Tax Supervisory and Conservation Committee hearing scheduled for June 9th and final adoption June 17th [00:10:18]. 

Multiple speakers criticized the budget process as lacking transparency, with one city employee noting the mayor's 1,141-page budget document removed key staffing comparison context from previous years. The core services realignment emerged as a major controversy, with union representatives calling it poorly planned and rushed.

**Real Estate Development Implications:** Budget constraints may affect permitting timelines and development review capacity as staffing cuts impact various bureaus.

### Public Safety  
Extensive testimony focused on proposed cuts to Portland Fire & Rescue and Portland Police Bureau. Isaac McLennan, president of Portland Firefighters Association, testified that PF&R currently reaches only 46.7% of the city within 4-minute response standards, with proposed cuts making coverage worse [00:42:08]. The closure of Engine 22 in St. Johns drew particular opposition from residents citing wildfire risks and geographic isolation.

Police Administrative Support Specialists testified about receiving layoff notices, describing critical functions including evidence management, personnel processing, and public interface services that would be eliminated. Public Safety Support Specialists (PS3s) face cuts from 38 to 8 positions under the proposed budget.

**Real Estate Development Implications:** Reduced emergency response capacity and longer response times could affect property values and development feasibility, particularly in areas like St. Johns and North Portland industrial districts.

### Climate & Sustainability Policy
Significant debate emerged over Portland Clean Energy Fund allocations, with multiple speakers opposing the use of PCF money for non-climate programs. Alan Hipólito from SUMA emphasized that PCF investments must meet five criteria including community leadership, which he argued many proposed uses lack [01:13:25]. 

Tree canopy protection dominated climate testimony, with speakers supporting amendments to restore 12 PCF-funded urban forestry positions that the mayor proposed cutting. Urban Forestry permitting staff testified about tree code enforcement needs amid invasive species threats and extreme weather events.

**Real Estate Development Implications:** Changes to tree permitting staffing could affect development timelines and requirements. Proper allocation of PCF funds may impact availability of climate-related development incentives.

### System Development Charges (SDCs) & Permitting Fees
Limited direct discussion of SDCs, though multiple references to "SDCs" appeared in amendment titles and testimony. One speaker mentioned construction workforce services funding tied to SDCs and Regional Workforce Equity Agreement (RWEA) requirements [01:29:05].

Technical amendments included corrections to permitting position authority, suggesting ongoing challenges in development services staffing. Tree permitting specifically was highlighted as potentially moving from Urban Forestry to PP&D, concerning urban forestry experts.

**Real Estate Development Implications:** Staffing changes in permitting could affect review timelines. Construction workforce requirements remain tied to SDC and RWEA frameworks.

### Real Estate & Housing
Housing policy received limited direct discussion, though the meeting opened with Loretta Smith challenging Council President Dunphy about an indefinitely postponed housing ordinance intended to support community-based homeownership developers [00:10:24]. The ordinance would have allocated funds to Williams and Russell Self Enhancement Inc. and Oasis of Praise for building purchases.

Several speakers referenced the Moderate Income Revolving Loan (MIRL) program and various housing support services facing cuts, though specific impacts were not detailed.

**Real Estate Development Implications:** Delays in housing policy implementation could affect affordable development pipelines. Community-based homeownership initiatives remain stalled.

### Homelessness & Shelter Policy
Multiple speakers addressed homeless services cuts, with particular concern about Urban Alchemy contract reductions and shelter capacity. One speaker in recovery supported maintaining Urban Alchemy's contract, noting their effectiveness compared to county providers [01:22:07]. 

Workforce development programs for people transitioning out of homelessness faced elimination under Portland Solutions reorganization, with advocates requesting separate line items for these services through RFP processes.

**Real Estate Development Implications:** Continued homelessness service gaps may affect development feasibility in certain areas and property management considerations.
