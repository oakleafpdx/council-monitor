## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The council addressed two major items: establishing a sister city relationship with Lviv, Ukraine, and appropriating $2.5 million for Black homeownership development projects. The meeting demonstrated notable collaboration across the council, with multiple members working together on the funding amendment. **Loretta Smith (District 1)** emphasized the personal significance of the homeownership investment, noting her family's own displacement history from Vanport and the opportunity to build generational wealth [02:30:55]. ## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### Real Estate & Housing
The council appropriated $2.5 million for affordable homeownership development targeting Black Portlanders in historically displaced communities. **Loretta Smith (District 1)** noted that Black homeownership rates in Portland are 25-28% compared to 57% for white residents [01:08:02]. The funding supports 49 net-zero homes through Self Enhancement Inc.'s Ruby Mac Place project and 20 townhomes through Williams Russell CDC's Knott Street development [01:15:52]. Both projects utilize Portland Housing Bureau's preference policy for displaced residents and include wraparound services. **Elana Pirtle-Guiney (District 2)** emphasized this represents "repair" for past displacement policies [02:33:06]. The amendment resolving funding source concerns demonstrates council commitment to both homeownership assistance and social housing development without creating competition between programs.

### City Budget & Finance
The amended ordinance shifts $2.5 million from the proposed revolving loan and social housing acquisition fund to the construction excise tax subfund [02:26:13]. **Sameer Kanal (District 2)** explained this change prevents taking resources from social housing while still supporting homeownership development. The construction excise tax represents a viable alternative funding source that maintains program integrity for both initiatives. This budget solution received support from diverse stakeholders including Portland Metro Chamber and Welcome Home Coalition, demonstrating broad consensus on the funding approach.

### Economic Development  
Multiple speakers emphasized homeownership as critical for building generational wealth and economic mobility. Andrew Holm from Portland Metro Chamber noted homeowners have median net worth of $430,000 versus $10,000 for renters [01:32:26]. Trent Aldridge from Self Enhancement Inc. cited Federal Reserve data showing homeowners have 40 times the net worth of renters [01:21:00]. The projects specifically target wealth-building opportunities for communities harmed by past displacement policies, with **Loretta Smith (District 1)** connecting this to her family's own 66-year homeownership history in Portland [02:30:55].

### Climate & Sustainability Policy
Both homeownership projects incorporate net-zero energy design standards. Williams Russell's Knott Street development will be built to Earth Advantage Platinum standards with collective net-zero performance supported by Portland Clean Energy Fund [01:15:52]. Self Enhancement Inc.'s Ruby Mac Place features 1,500-1,800 square foot net-zero homes [01:21:00]. These sustainability requirements demonstrate integration of climate goals with affordable housing development while reducing long-term utility costs for homeowners.
