## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This work session focused on the Community and Economic Development Service Area budget for FY 2026-27, covering Portland Permitting & Development (PP&D), Portland Housing Bureau (PHB), and Bureau of Planning & Sustainability (BPS). Mayor Keith Wilson presided over the meeting with all council members present, implementing a strict 5-minute per question format to accommodate the extensive agenda.

The most contentious issue was significant cuts to the tree permitting program at PP&D, reducing staff from 26 to 14 FTE positions due to funding shortfalls that emerged after transferring the program from Parks Bureau last year. Multiple councilors, particularly Steve Novick (District 3) [00:18:54] and Mitch Green (District 4) [00:32:02], strongly criticized using Portland Clean Energy Fund (PCF) dollars for other projects while cutting tree services, with Green stating he would not "tolerate cuts to the tree code people" [00:34:33]. The program's service delays have increased from 2 weeks to 6-7 weeks since the transfer [00:46:13].

The Housing Bureau presentation was relatively straightforward, with Director Michael Bonacor noting minimal cuts and maintained service levels. However, Eric Zimmerman (District 4) questioned whether this was an opportunity for more substantial reforms given the bureau's recent challenges [00:21:23]. The bureau's $276.3 million budget includes $86 million in future commitments categorized as contingency.

BPS Director Eric Engstrom outlined cuts to area planning, digital inclusion grants, and climate programs while highlighting the dissolution of the Mt. Hood Cable Regulatory Commission. The bureau proposed modest waste rate increases, with most residential services seeing increases under 5%.

## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### Real Estate & Housing

**Discussion:** The Housing Bureau maintains its $276.3 million budget with minimal staffing reductions and no service cuts. Director Michael Bonacor highlighted continued investment in 18 projects representing 1,800+ housing units, homeownership counseling for 1,000 households, and various tenant services [01:11:04].

**Key Issues Raised:**
- Dan Ryan (District 2) questioned the lack of longitudinal oversight metrics for housing developments, noting quality-of-life complaints from residents [01:27:36]
- Eric Zimmerman (District 4) challenged whether this was an opportunity for more substantial bureau reforms given recent management issues [00:21:23]
- Loretta Smith (District 1) inquired about the Home Share program's slow rollout, with $500,000 supporting up to 350 renters [01:33:23]

**Implications:** Housing production continues with stable funding, but oversight concerns may drive future policy changes affecting development accountability and tenant services.

### System Development Charges (SDCs) & Permitting Fees

**Discussion:** PP&D proposed targeted fee increases to maintain service levels, with most programs seeing 5% increases. Building mechanical permits would increase 9%, while accessory short-term rentals face 26% increases and noise permits 41% increases due to current below-cost-recovery status [00:18:25].

**Key Points:**
- Fee comparison data shows Portland falls within range of comparable jurisdictions [00:18:25]
- Director Eric Kooch emphasized predictable timelines matter as much as reasonable fees for development feasibility [00:18:25]

**Implications:** Moderate fee increases should not significantly impact development costs, but permitting delays from staff cuts may affect project timelines more substantially.

### Land Use & Zoning

**Discussion:** BPS will prioritize the Inner East rezoning project delivery but reduce capacity for other area planning work due to budget constraints [01:58:30]. The Code Alignment Project 2 (CAP 2) continues with multi-bureau coordination to streamline development regulations.

**Key Issues:**
- Area planning team cuts will limit neighborhood-specific planning capacity
- Dan Ryan (District 2) emphasized importance of maintaining regulatory reform momentum built over recent years [01:40:52]

**Implications:** Core zoning reform continues, but reduced planning capacity may slow neighborhood-level development policy updates.

### City Budget & Finance

**Discussion:** The Community and Economic Development Service Area represents $1.235 billion in total budget authority (including PCF reserves), with 695.3 proposed staff positions across all bureaus [00:07:36].

**Key Financial Issues:**
- Tree permitting funding gap of approximately $2.9 million causing program cuts
- Debate over PCF fund allocation priorities, with $75 million proposed for Moda Center versus tree program needs
- Housing Bureau maintains stable funding with 70% of service area budget going directly to community support

**Implications:** Budget pressures create tension between infrastructure investments and direct services, potentially affecting development support services.

### Climate & Sustainability Policy

**Discussion:** BPS climate programs face general fund cuts reducing contractor capacity for technical support, affecting Home Energy Score program assistance and climate analysis capabilities [01:59:35]. However, the bureau maintains core sustainability functions including renewable fuels transition and energy utility coordination.

**Key Changes:**
- Reduced technical assistance for Home Energy Score compliance
- Limited participation in peer city climate networks
- Loss of grant-funded position due to multi-year grant ending

**Implications:** Development projects may receive less technical support for energy efficiency requirements, potentially slowing green building adoption.

### Economic Development

**Discussion:** The service area includes Prosper Portland as a key partner, though not formally a city bureau. The area focuses on economic development strategy advancement and real estate development activation [00:07:36].

**Implications:** Continued economic development coordination should support development-friendly policies and business attraction efforts.
