## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This budget committee meeting focused on voting on amendments to Portland's FY2026-27 proposed budget, with 12 amendments considered during the session. The meeting was characterized by significant tension around funding sources and competing priorities, with several councilors expressing frustration about the use of contingency funds and "magic money" to fund proposals.

Mayor Keith Wilson and councilors engaged in heated debate over homelessness policy, with amendments to reduce sweep funding and redirect resources toward sanitation and workforce development programs. Mitch Green (District 4) successfully passed an amendment [00:16:53] cutting $1 million from the Impact Reduction Program (sweeps) budget, despite strong opposition from Mayor Wilson who argued that Portland now offers shelter at each removal and has "turned the corner" [00:18:28].

The meeting also addressed multiple parks and arts restoration amendments, with several proposals failing due to concerns about drawing from parks levy out-years or general fund contingency. Jonas Biery (CFO / Deputy City Administrator for Budget & Finance) repeatedly warned against overuse of contingency funds [00:48:59], while councilors like Elana Pirtle-Guiney (District 2) expressed "zero tolerance" for pulling from future levy years [01:41:04].

The session ran late into the evening, with Council President Jamie Dunphy (District 1) frequently urging brevity as they worked through amendments under time pressure. The tone became increasingly tense, culminating in Steve Novick (District 3) sarcastically proposing to buy ponies for children with "magic money" [00:56:41] to highlight his frustration with unfunded proposals.

## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### Real Estate & Housing
Candace Avalos (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) led efforts to reform Portland's homelessness response system through governance restructuring [00:33:41]. Her failed amendments would have created dedicated planning capacity to evaluate shelter operations, reduce city-county duplication, and redirect resources toward permanent housing solutions. The proposals aimed to address budget gaps by forcing decisions about sustainable system funding before the next budget cycle.

### System Development Charges (SDCs) & Permitting Fees  
SDCs were referenced multiple times as a constrained funding source. Mitch Green (District 4) noted that arts grants took a 7% cut overall [01:24:34], with System Development Charges specifically mentioned as an alternative funding mechanism for parks capital projects like the Montevilla shelter [02:04:33].

### Homelessness & Shelter Policy
Major debate centered on Portland's sweep policies and shelter system. Mitch Green (District 4) argued that the city exceeded its sweep targets, conducting 8,303 sweeps in FY25 versus a 4,000 target [00:11:03], creating "terror and fear" among homeless individuals. Mayor Wilson countered that Portland is "one of only a handful of cities" offering shelter at each removal [00:18:28] and that the city has "turned the corner" with homelessness declining in polls from 53% to 33% concern.

The Impact Reduction Program budget totals $14.955 million [00:24:48], with Taser settlement requirements mandating minimum $3 million annual spending through 2028. Successful cuts will eliminate 3-4 removal crews of 2-3 people each [00:45:52].

### City Budget & Finance
Jonas Biery (CFO / Deputy City Administrator for Budget & Finance) repeatedly warned against overuse of general fund contingency [00:48:59]. Multiple amendments drew from parks levy out-years, with councilors split on this practice. The meeting highlighted ongoing structural budget challenges, with Candace Avalos noting this is "year 2 of staring down huge holes in our budget" [00:42:01].

Council operations received $374,000 for potential post-election transitions [01:28:14], which was successfully redirected to arts funding with an underspending backfill mechanism.

### Infrastructure & Transportation
Limited discussion of infrastructure, primarily focused on parks capital needs. The Montevilla Park shelter project represents deferred capital maintenance, with the original structure demolished in October 2021 for health and safety reasons [01:58:52]. The replacement has been stalled due to budget constraints despite being originally planned in a previous parks levy.
