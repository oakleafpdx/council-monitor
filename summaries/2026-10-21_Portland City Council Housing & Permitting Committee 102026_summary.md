## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Agenda.** The committee heard three things. It approved September minutes, took general community feedback on housing and permitting (Item 2, doc 2026-376), and held the second hearing on the **Climate and Health Standard** (Item 3, doc 2026-377). That ordinance would replace Code Chapter 17.104. It adds multifamily annual energy reporting, minimum carbon performance standards for large existing commercial and multifamily buildings, and energy-cost and appliance disclosures to prospective tenants. **No vote was taken on the ordinance.** Candace Avalos (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) said she would bring it back December 1, "hopefully for a vote" [03:02:35].

**Presentation and testimony.** Damon Motz Storey, the Chair's new Director of Policy and Communications, recapped the policy [00:22:06]. He said buildings account for 42% of city emissions [00:22:39]. The state building performance standard would address only about 5% of emissions from Portland's covered buildings, while the city standard would cover about 40% of building-sector emissions [00:26:08]. Targets step down 20% by 2030 and another 20% every five years to zero by 2050 [00:30:05]. Regulated affordable housing is exempt, and there is a carbon pollution impact fee for off-track buildings [00:30:05]. More than 40 people signed up to testify, limited to two minutes each [00:21:23]. Testimony ran roughly 00:34 to 02:18 and was sharply divided.

- **Opposed or seeking a pause:** Multifamily Northwest, a BOMA/CREDC/Oregon Smart Growth/Metro Chamber coalition, Northwest Natural and the Northwest Gas Association, building trades representatives, hospitals (Providence, Legacy), universities (OHSU, PSU, PCC, OAICU), the Port of Portland (technical requests), Oregon Restaurant & Lodging Association, and small and mid-size owners.
- **Supported:** the City Sustainability and Climate Commission, Coalition of Communities of Color, Oregon Physicians for Social Responsibility, 350 PDX, Earth Advantage, Portland for All, and individual tenants and residents.

**Councilor discussion.** Only four of five members were present. Steve Novick (District 3) asked detailed questions on baselines, naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH) costs, funding capacity, new buildings, vacancy, and BPS administrative cost, and asked to hear from New York City implementers [02:44:05]. Mitch Green (District 4) expressed strong support and called many opposition arguments "strawman arguments" [02:54:20]. Tiffany Koyama Lane (District 3) invited the city's Chief Sustainability Officer, Vivian Satterfield, to comment [02:59:32]. Pirtle-Guiney's chief of staff, Devin Mullins, read prepared remarks supporting ambitious climate action while raising questions on contingency plans if utilities miss clean-energy targets, definitions, and enforcement discretion for hospital campuses [02:20:39].

**Tone.** The chair engaged opponents point by point and defended the process and flexibility provisions [02:40:10]. A testifier was ejected for an offensive slur [01:13:21]. Because the chair wants a clean revised draft rather than a "big amendment drama" [03:02:35], expect changes before December 1, with higher education, hospital campuses, and utility/economic analysis the most likely pressure points.

## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### Real Estate & Housing (extra detail)

**Policy mechanics as presented**
- Motz Storey said the ordinance requires owners to track and disclose energy-cost peaks and averages, available utility-assistance programs, and appliance types (cooling, gas furnace vs. heat pump) before lease signing [00:26:08].
- He said the policy bars passing retrofit costs and fees through to tenants. It also includes relocation support and a right to return to the same unit [00:30:05].
- He said regulated affordable housing is exempt, and he cited the Eco Northwest study as finding "no net impact" on rent plus utilities for tenants in upgraded buildings [00:30:05].
- The chair confirmed the policy covers **existing buildings only**; new construction is handled under a separate set of conditions [02:53:13].
- Vivek Shandas (Sustainability and Climate Commission) said each building is measured against its own baseline so the worst performers (often affordable housing) don't face the biggest jump. He also said most multifamily upgrades would come around 2040–2045 [00:35:46].

**Owner and operator concerns**
- David Thompson (Multifamily Northwest) said the policy imposes unfunded retrofit mandates. He said owners pay fees on emissions from tenant behavior they can't control, and he cited the city's housing needs analysis of about 120,000 homes by 2045 [00:56:11].
- Jonathan Clay (Multifamily Northwest) said the July 14 draft was released the same day as the only public meeting, and he asked that decarbonization provisions be separated from "unfunded mandatory building retrofits" [00:43:10].
- Madeline Miller (BOMA/CREDC/Oregon Smart Growth/Metro Chamber coalition) said the city's own analysis shows the highest per-unit costs for older naturally occurring affordable housing owned by small landlords. She warned this could accelerate consolidation and sales [00:49:53].
- Tyler Reeves (small landlord and developer) cited a city-consultant estimate of more than $10,000 per unit for older buildings just to meet the first compliance step. He said the assistance fund is filled by penalties, so help "arrives after the owners have already failed." He also said relocation costs fall on owners and penalties are uncapped and can include limits on building permits [02:10:27].
- Malcolm McIver (Commerce Properties) described a NOAH building where rents rose 9% over five years, payroll rose 31%, utilities rose 32%, and insurance rose 182%. He warned a mandate could drive such buildings into bankruptcy [01:46:27].
- Jordan Vasquez (Deacon Development) said rushing the policy would reduce already-low production of unsubsidized middle- and lower-income housing [02:04:11].
- Megan Howard (developer/owner of a 2020 building) asked what a new, already efficient building would retrofit. She also raised the occupancy-baseline issue, where higher occupancy raises energy use [01:22:56].
- Jim Marquardt (owner of two 1920s brick buildings) spent $500,000 on windows and asked how that effort is credited. He warned against repeating the URM-upgrade experience [01:16:24].

**Tenant and homeowner perspectives**
- Sarah Wood (District 4 tenant) said she pays roughly $300 a month to heat to 68°F and supports cost transparency and landlord incentives [02:09:14].
- Rachel Chide asked that the disclosures extend to prospective low-income homeowners and that condo and townhome owners get support [01:13:34].
- Lacey Sutton said Habitat for Humanity's construction damaged a large Douglas fir's root system and an arborist deemed it imminently likely to fail. She asked the city for immediate action [00:08:29]. The chair did not respond on substance beyond follow-up [00:17:27].
- Amber Cook alleged misconduct at state and regional housing agencies and called for ending "golden parachutes" [00:14:23]. These are the testifier's claims and not verified in the meeting.

**Chair's responses and Novick's questions**
- The chair said the policy includes extensions and exemptions at Section 17.104.170 (nine anticipated situations, including undue hardship such as bankruptcy). She also said the baseline-year flexibility credits owners who have already invested [02:40:10].
- BPS staff Ben Mason said multifamily owners have no existing reporting history, though state standards may make three years of data available. Commercial buildings 20,000+ sq ft have reported since 2015 [02:46:30].
- BPS said 2030 is a "procedural compliance year" with no building changes expected, so retrofits are back-end loaded [02:47:33].
- Novick asked for NOAH-specific cost analysis for 2035, 2040, and 2045 and for the share of overall conversion cost PCEF and the Energy Trust of Oregon could cover [02:49:48].
- BPS's director (rendered "Eric King's term" in the transcript; likely Eric Engstrom, verify) said the city paused work in 2024 to stand up the PCEF program aimed at naturally occurring affordable housing [02:51:22].

**Implications for net-zero infill multifamily:**
- New all-electric buildings are not the target of this ordinance, but buildings eventually become "existing" stock. Confirm how and when covered-building status and baselines attach (20,000 sq ft threshold). The chair said new construction is handled separately [02:53:13].
- Owners would carry the reporting and tenant-disclosure obligations, and these could affect operating cost and underwriting.
- The Novick and Reeves exchanges show an unresolved gap on NOAH retrofit costs. Expect amendments or added analysis before December 1.

### Climate & Sustainability Policy (core item)

- The chair said buildings are 42% of Portland's emissions, electricity bills are up about 50% over five years, and the policy supports the Climate Emergency Work Plan's goal of zero emissions by 2050 [00:18:12].
- Shandas said the Commission endorsed the standards August 20 but noted the draft lacks maximum indoor temperature standards [00:35:46].
- Vivian Satterfield (Chief Sustainability Officer) said building emissions must be addressed and described the policy as complementary to the state standard [03:00:09].
- Mitch Green (District 4) called state policy "not very aggressive" and said cities must lead. He tied snowpack loss and hydropower to energy costs [02:54:20].
- Opponents said the Eco Northwest study reviewed an earlier version and needs updating (Amy Rothfelder [00:52:07]; Michael Myers [01:04:00]). They also said the city should wait for or align with state building performance standards (Greg Astley, ORLA [02:02:05]; Randall Friesen [01:09:47]).
- Opponents argued the policy is effectively all-electric and technology-restrictive. Ross Kelly (Menashe Properties) said it allows no offsets or renewable natural gas [01:39:29]. Jason Verduzco (Northwest Gas Association) asked the committee to defer action [01:53:08].
- Dan Eisenbeiss (Port of Portland) asked that "covered building" exclude buildings predominantly used for aviation and that the "owner" definition clarify ground-lease situations [01:37:21].
- Hospitals and campuses asked for a defined, amendable alternative compliance pathway (Legacy, [00:40:50]; Providence, Andy Mason [00:38:37]). OHSU, PSU, PCC, and OAICU asked for the higher-ed exemption already given to K-12 (Greg Mullawad [00:45:27]; Sarah Heinecke [01:01:01]; Allison Krauss [02:14:01]; Cindy Robert [00:47:37]).
- **Next steps:** Chair to review the "deluge of letters," work with BPS and attorneys on amendments, and return December 1 [03:02:35].
