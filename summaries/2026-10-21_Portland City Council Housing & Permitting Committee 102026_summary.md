## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The committee met on two items. The first was a routine approval of September meeting minutes, followed by general community feedback (Item 2, doc. 2026-376). The second, and by far the larger, was **Item 3, doc. 2026-377, the Climate and Health Standard**. This ordinance would replace Code Chapter 17.104, adding carbon performance standards and expanded energy reporting for commercial and multifamily buildings (covering 20,000+ sq ft). It would also add tenant energy-cost and appliance disclosures. **No vote was taken.** After a staff recap, 40+ testifiers (2 minutes each), and a short deliberation, Candace Avalos (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) said the item will return on **December 1** "for hopefully a vote," with refinements made beforehand [03:02:35].

The policy case was framed by the chair and her policy director, Damon Motz Storey. Their points:
- Buildings account for 42% of Portland's emissions [00:22:31].
- The state standard would reach only ~5% of covered-building emissions, versus ~40% for the city proposal [00:26:08].
- Reductions would be 20% by 2030 and an additional 20% every five years, reaching 100% by 2050 [00:30:03].
- Regulated affordable housing is exempt, and there is a carbon pollution impact fee for off-track buildings [00:30:05].
- Tenants get relocation help, a right to return, and no pass-through of retrofit costs [00:30:05].

Testimony split sharply:
- **Opposed or asking for a pause:**
  - Multifamily Northwest, a business/real estate coalition (BOMA, CREDA, Oregon Smart Growth, Portland Metro Chamber), and NW Natural / NW Gas Association.
  - Building trades unions, hospitals (Providence/Legacy), and higher education (OHSU, PSU, PCC, OAICU).
  - Small landlords.
- **Supportive:** Coalition of Communities of Color, Oregon Physicians for Social Responsibility, 350 PDX, the City Sustainability and Climate Commission, Earth Advantage, Portland for All, and individual tenants and residents.

The main objections were:
- Process and speed (the July 14 release).
- An outdated Eco Northwest economic analysis.
- Unfunded retrofit mandates.
- Duplication of state building performance standards.
- Owners penalized for tenant-controlled energy use.
- Baselines set while buildings are vacant.
- Grid reliability.

Committee members present were supportive in tone but wanted more information. Steve Novick (District 3) asked for New York City implementation experts, cost modeling for naturally occurring affordable housing (NOAH) through 2045, and the cost of flexibility to the city [02:44:05]. Mitch Green (District 4) expressed strong support [02:54:39]. Tiffany Koyama Lane (District 3) invited the Chief Sustainability Officer to speak [02:59:32]. Elana Pirtle-Guiney (District 2) was absent with strep throat, and her chief of staff read her priorities and questions [02:18:53].

The tone was contentious but largely orderly. One testifier used an ableist slur and was removed [01:13:26]. Another disability-community testifier thanked the chair for calling it out [01:13:34].

## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### Real Estate & Housing *(emphasis topic)*

**What the ordinance does for multifamily (Damon Motz Storey, policy director to Chair Avalos [00:22:06]):**
- **Reporting:** Adds annual energy reporting for multifamily residential, extending the existing commercial requirement [00:26:08].
- **Tenant disclosure:** Owners must disclose energy-cost peaks and averages, available assistance programs, and unit appliances (central AC or cooling, gas furnace vs. heat pump) [00:26:08].
- **Compliance flexibility:** Owners can choose a baseline year and use alternative compliance pathways. Regulated affordable housing is exempt [00:30:05].
- **Tenant protections:** Temporary relocation provisions, a right to return, and no pass-through of retrofit costs or fees to tenants [00:30:05].
- **Economic study:** The Eco Northwest study found no net impact on rent plus utilities for tenants in upgraded buildings [00:30:05].

**What the chair said about scope:** The ordinance covers **existing buildings only**. New construction is purposefully not addressed, because it is governed by a separate set of conditions [02:52:54].

**Opposition from housing providers:**
- **Jonathan Clay, Multifamily Northwest [00:43:10]:**
  - Said the process was "blazingly fast," with the policy released July 14, the same day as the only public meeting.
  - Asked to separate decarbonization provisions from unfunded mandatory retrofits.
- **David Thompson, Multifamily Northwest [00:56:11]:**
  - Said a May 2024 BPS letter stated "no upgrades to buildings will be required."
  - Said owners are penalized for tenant energy use they cannot control.
  - Cited the city's housing needs analysis of ~120,000 homes by 2045.
  - Warned the policy could divert capital from maintenance, push up rents, and risk building closures.
- **Madeline Miller (lobbyist for BOMA, CREDA, Oregon Smart Growth, Portland Metro Chamber) [00:49:53]:**
  - Said the city's own analysis shows per-unit costs are highest for older naturally occurring affordable housing, owned by small landlords.
  - Warned of market consolidation.
- **Malcolm McIver, Commerce Properties [01:46:27]:**
  - Gave one NOAH building's five-year numbers: rents +9%, payroll +31%, utilities +32%, insurance +182%.
  - Warned that a mandated capex could push such buildings toward bankruptcy.
- **Jim Marquardt, owner of two 1920s brick buildings [01:16:24]:**
  - Cited a $500,000 window upgrade already completed.
  - Raised the earlier URM seismic mandate experience and the unfunded-mandate concern.
- **Megan Howard, developer and housing provider [01:22:56]:**
  - Asked what a 2020 NGBS-certified building is supposed to retrofit.
  - Raised occupancy-driven energy increases after the baseline is set.
  - Asked the city to complete its landlord-tenant study first.
- **Jordan Vasquez, Deacon Development [02:04:11]:**
  - Argued the policy would shrink already-thin unsubsidized middle and lower-income supply.
- **Tyler Reeves, small landlord and Spec Development [02:10:27]:**
  - Cited consultant estimates of more than $10,000 per unit for older units to reach the first compliance step.
  - Said relocation costs fall entirely on owners.
  - Said penalties are uncapped, including limits on building permits.
  - Said Denver is extending deadlines and halving fines.

**Support and tenant-side input:**
- **Samantha Hernandez, Coalition of Communities of Color [01:00:00]:** Urged a yes vote on behalf of 28 organizations.
- **Sarah Wood, tenant [02:09:14]:** Described ~$300/month heating costs in a poorly insulated building.
- **Rachel Chide (online) [01:13:34]:** Asked that disclosure also reach prospective low-income homebuyers, and that support cover condo and townhome owners.

**Other housing/construction matters raised in community feedback:**
- **Lacey Sutton [00:08:29]:** Described a 125-foot Douglas fir she said was damaged by nearby Habitat for Humanity construction, with an arborist calling failure "imminent" and a prior city code complaint dismissed. The chair promised follow-up [00:17:27]. This is a reminder of liability and inspection exposure on infill sites.
- **Amber Cook [00:14:23]:** Raised misconduct allegations about state and regional housing agency leaders.

**Implications for net-zero infill multifamily developers:**
- New buildings are not directly regulated by this ordinance [02:52:54]. Newly built, all-electric product is structurally well-positioned.
- **Hold-period exposure:** Owners who hold stabilized assets would face reporting, the baseline-year choice, and the 2050 zero-emissions endpoint.
- **Vacancy and lease-up:** Baseline-and-occupancy mechanics could matter for assets still leasing up. Avalos said Alternative Compliance Plans address vacancy [02:53:27].
- **Disclosure:** The tenant energy-cost and appliance disclosure requirement is a leasing and marketing item to watch.
- **Revisions pending:** Texts of owner/tenant responsibility and baseline provisions may change before Dec. 1.

### Climate & Sustainability Policy *(core topic)*

**Staff/sponsor case:**
- **Avalos (opening) [00:18:12]:**
  - 42% of the city's carbon footprint comes from heating, cooling, and powering buildings.
  - Electricity bills are up ~50% over five years.
  - The 2021 heat dome killed dozens, many without cooling.
  - State rules require the two big electric utilities to be emissions-free by 2040.
- **Avalos (deliberation) [02:40:10]:**
  - The baseline-year flexibility gives credit for prior progress.
  - The long 2030–2050 runway is meant to let owners replace equipment during normal upkeep.
  - She described the ordinance's Alternative Compliance Plans as providing flexibility for long-lived equipment.
  - She walked through the redline document: a three-column comparison covering the 2022 version, 2025 changes before her involvement, and her office's changes.
  - Section 17.104.170 (extensions/exemptions) lists nine anticipated situations, including hardship and bankruptcy.
  - She asserted the grid can handle electrification.

**Supporters:**
- **Vivek Shandas, City Sustainability & Climate Commission [00:35:46]:**
  - The commission voted Aug. 20 to endorse the standards.
  - The pilot showed reporting takes a few hours in year one and under 2 hours after.
  - He noted the proposal lacks maximum indoor temperature standards and supported taking that up next.
- **Cara Saylor, Green Energy Institute [01:25:13]:** Said Portland is "a laggard" relative to 4 states, 11 cities, and 1 county.
- **Dr. Anne Turner and Elana Trucker, Oregon Physicians for Social Responsibility [01:27:48], [01:48:42]:** Health arguments on gas stoves and weatherization.
- **Charisse Bach, 350 PDX [01:55:34]; Andres Oswald, Portland for All [01:57:56]:** Supported the policy as drafted, urging against added carve-outs.
- **Earth Advantage's David [01:32:40]:** Said rulemaking can resolve details, and said the policy permits fossil-fuel backup generators.

**Council members:**
- **Mitch Green (District 4) [02:54:39]:**
  - Fully supportive.
  - Argued that "not aligned with the state" is not a compelling critique because the state standard is not aggressive enough.
  - Called many opposition claims "strawman arguments."
- **Vivian Satterfield, Chief Sustainability Officer [03:00:09]:**
  - Said regulatory frameworks for buildings are "inevitable."
  - Said the commission valued the pairing of decarbonization with tenant protections.
- **Elana Pirtle-Guiney (District 2), via chief of staff Devin Mullins [02:20:39]:**
  - She supports ambitious climate goals.
  - Her questions: contingency if electric utilities miss clean-energy targets, how ordinance definitions apply, and how city discretion on enforcement will apply to unique buildings such as hospital campuses.

**Opposition on climate substance:**
- **NW Natural (Dundon [01:18:52], Yoakam [00:54:04], Myers [01:04:00], Rothfelder [00:52:07]) and Northwest Gas Association (Verduzco [01:53:08]):**
  - Resilience concerns.
  - The Eco Northwest study evaluated an earlier version.
  - Requests to pause and consider RNG, hydrogen, hybrid, and district energy.
- **Hospitals and higher education:**
  - Legacy Health [00:40:50]: Asked for a pause and a clearer, amendable campus compliance pathway; cited near-$1B planning needs.
  - Providence (Andy Mason) [00:38:37]: Raised resilience of systems that must stay online.

**Next steps:** Revisions are expected before Dec. 1.
