## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The committee held two agenda items. **Item 2 (Doc 2026-376)** was general Housing and Permitting community feedback, with three speakers [00:08:01]. **Item 3 (Doc 2026-377)** was the **Climate and Health Standard**, which would replace Code Chapter 17.104. It adds building-code climate and health standards to reduce carbon emissions from commercial and multifamily buildings and to give renters information on energy cost and health [00:17:45]. Before the main item, the committee approved its September minutes by unanimous consent [00:07:24]. **No vote on the ordinance occurred in the portion of the transcript provided.**

Chair Candace Avalos (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) framed the policy as a housing-affordability and public-health measure. She cited roughly 50% electricity-bill growth over five years, the 2021 heat dome, and the finding that buildings account for 42% of the city's carbon footprint [00:18:12]. She also said state performance standards fall short and that Portland's standards would cover about 40% of building-sector emissions [00:18:12]. Her new Director of Policy and Communications, Damon Motz Storey, gave the staff-style recap [00:22:06]. Testimony was capped at 2 minutes because more than 40 people had signed up [00:21:23]. Roll call named Mitch Green (District 4), Elana Pirtle-Guiney (District 2), Tiffany Koyama Lane (District 3), Steve Novick (District 3) and Candace Avalos (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) [00:06:04–00:06:14]. Testifiers repeatedly addressed Mitch Green (District 4) as Vice Chair [00:35:46]. **No councilor other than the chair is recorded speaking substantively in this transcript.** One testifier said his District 2 councilor was not in the room [01:32:40], and the presiding speaker replied that the councilor's chief of staff was present [01:35:02].

**The testimony split sharply along industry lines.**
- **Opponents** asked for a pause, updated economic analysis, alignment with Oregon's state Building Performance Standards, or exemptions. ## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### Real Estate & Housing *(emphasis topic)*

**Policy framing and housing-cost angle**
- Candace Avalos (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) called the standards a housing policy. She said they address a lack of up-front information on a unit's energy costs and cooling for renters, and said Portland electricity bills rose about 50% in five years [00:18:12].
- Damon Motz Storey (Chair's Director of Policy & Communications) summarized the three ordinance components [00:26:08]:
  1. Annual energy reporting extended to multifamily residential buildings.
  2. Minimum carbon performance standards.
  3. Tenant disclosure of energy cost peaks and averages, available assistance programs, and appliance types (cooling, gas furnace vs. heat pump).
- On carbon targets, he said owners can pick a baseline year and there are alternative compliance pathways. Reductions start at 20% in 2030, with an additional 20% every 5 years [00:26:08].
- On exemptions and fees, he said regulated affordable housing is exempt, citing PCEF grants and similar programs. A carbon pollution impact fee applies to buildings that are off track [00:30:05].
- On tenant protections, he said the ordinance includes temporary-relocation support, a right to return to the same unit, and language that retrofit and fee costs should not be passed through to tenants. He said the Eco Northwest study found no net impact on rent plus utilities [00:30:05].
- Vivek Shandas (City Sustainability and Climate Commission) said the commission endorsed the standards on August 20. He said each building is measured against its own baseline, so worst-performing (often affordable) buildings don't face the biggest jump, and that owners can draw on Energy Trust and PCEF funding [00:35:46].
- Vivek Shandas also said the current proposal lacks **maximum indoor temperature standards**, which the commission wants taken up as a next step [00:35:46].

**Industry concerns tied directly to housing production**
- David Thompson (Multifamily Northwest) said the city's housing needs analysis calls for about 120,000 homes by 2045 and that the ordinance would divert capital from maintenance, raise rents, and risk building closures [00:56:11]. He also said:
  - A May 2024 BPS letter had said no building upgrades would be required, and the July 14 draft was the first to require them [00:56:11].
  - Owners are fined on whole-building emissions, including tenants' in-unit energy use, which owners can't control [00:56:11].
- Jonathan Clay (Multifamily Northwest) asked the committee to separate the decarbonization provisions from "unfunded mandatory building retrofits." He said the July 14 release coincided with the only public meeting and that Seattle's comparable policy took 2 years [00:43:10].
- Madeline Miller (lobbyist for BOMA, CREDA, Oregon Smart Growth and the Portland Metro Chamber) cited the city's own analysis. She said per-unit costs are highest for older naturally occurring affordable housing owned by small landlords, which could accelerate sales and consolidation [00:51:11].
- Malcolm McIver (Commerce Properties) gave a sample NOAH building's five-year numbers [01:46:27]:

  | Item | Five-year change |
  |------|------------------|
  | Rents | +9% |
  | Payroll | +31% |
  | Utilities | +32% |
  | Insurance | +182% |

  He warned a mandated capital expense or penalty could push such buildings into bankruptcy [01:46:27].
- Jim Marquardt, a small owner of two 1920s buildings, said he spent $500,000 on windows in one building. He asked whether such prior work counts, and compared the proposal to the URM seismic-mandate experience [01:16:24].
- Megan Howard, a developer and housing provider, said her 2020 community already includes efficiency measures and NGBS Green certification. She asked what a new building would retrofit, and warned that higher occupancy after a baseline year could raise energy use [01:22:56]. She also asked that the city's landlord-tenant study be completed and made public before new regulation [01:22:56].
- Jordan Vasquez (Deacon Development, naturally affordable/workforce housing) began testimony that rushing the policy raises costs for renters. **The transcript cuts off mid-statement** [02:04:11].

**Support and requested refinements**
- Samantha Hernandez (Coalition of Communities of Color) urged a yes vote on behalf of a 28-organization sign-on letter, citing 6–7 years of engagement [00:59:06].
- A testifier called up as "River" (self-identified as Rachel Chide) asked that energy-cost disclosure also be provided to prospective **low-income homebuyers**. They also asked for support for low-income condo and townhome owners not covered by the tenant protections [01:13:34].

**Other housing-related items**

**Relevance for a net-zero infill multifamily developer**

- The no-pass-through provision and tenant-disclosure requirements affect underwriting and leasing operations [00:30:05].
