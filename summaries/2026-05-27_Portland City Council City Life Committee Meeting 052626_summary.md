## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The City Life Committee held a productive meeting covering four main agenda items, with the most significant being a policy discussion on transportation network company (TNC) fare transparency and driver earnings standards. Elana Pirtle-Guiney (District 2) chaired the meeting, with Dan Ryan (District 2), Angelita Morillo (District 3), Eric Zimmerman (District 4), and Candace Avalos (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) in attendance.

The committee heard extensive testimony from rideshare drivers advocating for fare transparency and limits on how much Uber and Lyft can retain from passenger fares. Multiple drivers shared examples where companies kept 80% or more of passenger payments while drivers received as little as 16% of the fare [00:34:40]. The drivers union presented a coalition of over 45 organizations supporting "Fair Rides Oregon" legislation.

Professor James Parrott from the New School provided expert analysis showing that Portland Uber drivers earn approximately $12.05 per hour after expenses, which is 36% below what employers must pay minimum wage workers when including payroll taxes and benefits [01:00:00]. The committee also moved forward two practical items: removing permit requirements for portable sidewalk signs and finalizing the dissolution of the Mt. Hood Cable Regulatory Commission.

The meeting maintained a collaborative tone, with councilors asking detailed questions about TNC pricing models, permit processes, and regulatory frameworks. All formal votes passed unanimously, demonstrating committee consensus on the items considered.

## 2. KEY VOTES & ACTIONS

### Ordinance 2026-174: Portable Sign Permit Elimination
- **Action:** Moved to full council with recommendation to pass
- **Vote:** 5-0 (Ryan, Morillo, Zimmerman, Avalos, Pirtle-Guiney all aye)
- **Motion:** Eric Zimmerman (District 4) [01:36:21]
- **Second:** Dan Ryan (District 2) [01:36:22]
- **Significance:** Removes permit requirements for portable sidewalk signs (A-boards/sandwich boards), elimininating a $46,000 program that only generated $39,000 in revenue

### Ordinances 2026-175 & 2026-176: Mt. Hood Cable Regulatory Commission Dissolution
- **Action:** Both ordinances moved to full council with recommendation to pass
- **Vote:** 5-0 (Ryan, Morillo, Zimmerman, Avalos, Pirtle-Guiney all aye)
- **Motion:** Dan Ryan (District 2) [01:46:33]
- **Second:** Angelita Morillo (District 3) [01:46:35]
- **Significance:** Transfers cable franchise oversight back to City of Portland and establishes new Cable Franchise Fund (Fund 234) to replace MHCRC Fund 855

### TNC Fare Transparency Discussion
- **Action:** Information only, no formal vote
- **Next Steps:** Roundtable negotiations with Uber and Lyft starting June 18th; draft ordinance expected mid-July/August
- **Significance:** Potential establishment of "take rate" limits on how much TNCs can retain from passenger fares

## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### Real Estate & Housing
The TNC fare transparency discussion has significant implications for Portland's housing accessibility and transportation infrastructure. Elana Pirtle-Guiney (District 2) noted that TNCs "help folks live in our city without needing a car of their own" and "support our hospitality and tourism sectors" [00:30:58]. The policy could affect how residents without personal vehicles access employment and services, potentially impacting housing location decisions and development patterns near transit corridors.

### Economic Development
Multiple agenda items directly addressed small business support. The portable sign ordinance eliminates regulatory burden on small businesses, with Spencer Knowles noting that business districts requested the change because "compliance rate is fairly low" and the permit requirement was "a burden upon many of the small businesses in Portland" [00:22:04]. Eric Zimmerman (District 4) characterized this as attacking "dumb laws" and reducing Portland's reputation as "a city of permission slips" [01:27:55].

The TNC discussion also centers on supporting local drivers as small business owners while maintaining essential transportation infrastructure for Portland's economy.

### System Development Charges (SDCs) & Permitting Fees
The portable sign program analysis revealed broader issues with Portland's fee-based regulatory programs. The sign program showed a "reserve deficit of over $250,000" with the portable sign component losing $7,000 annually [01:32:06]. This indicates systemic challenges with self-supporting regulatory programs that may require General Fund subsidies.

### Infrastructure & Transportation
The TNC policy discussion focused extensively on transportation network regulation and driver compensation. Professor Parrott's analysis showed Portland drivers earn $12.05 per hour after expenses, compared to federal mileage deduction rates of 72.5 cents per mile while Portland pays only 70 cents per mile [01:00:00]. This has implications for maintaining adequate rideshare service levels and transportation accessibility.

### City Budget & Finance
The Cable Franchise Fund establishment creates a dedicated revenue stream for public, educational, and government (PEG) access programming. Andrew Speer noted the fund will allow strategic planning for "what is the use of PEG dollars over time" and ensure compliance with federal FCC guidance [01:44:50].
