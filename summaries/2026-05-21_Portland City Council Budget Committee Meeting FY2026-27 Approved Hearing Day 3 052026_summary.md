## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This was Day 3 of Portland's FY2026-27 budget committee hearings, described by Council President Jamie Dunphy (District 1, Council President) as the "long day" expected to run until 9-10 PM [00:08:08]. The meeting focused primarily on amendments to the Prosper Portland budget and Portland Clean Energy Fund (PCF) allocations, with significant debate over the appropriate use of voter-approved funds.

The session began with completing Prosper Portland amendments, including heated debate over Candace Avalos's (District 1, Housing & Permitting Committee Chair) proposal to move an immigrant affairs liaison position from the Mayor's office to the City Administrator's office, which failed 6-6. Jamie Dunphy (District 1, Council President) also unsuccessfully proposed an "East Portland Harmless" amendment drawing from various funding sources to restore positions and infrastructure investments.

The most contentious discussions centered on PCF fund usage, with multiple councilors arguing that Mayor Keith Wilson's proposed diversions of climate justice funds to sidewalk cleaning and public environment management violated voter intent. Both Candace Avalos (District 1) and Steve Novick (District 3) offered competing approaches to restore PCF integrity, but both failed on 6-6 votes. The meeting also addressed urban forestry staffing cuts, with several amendments proposed to restore tree permitting positions using PCF contingency funds.

The council struggled with multiple 6-6 vote splits, indicating deep divisions on budget priorities. Tensions were particularly high around immigration services, with Candace Avalos (District 1) stating she would "make sure everyone knows what just happened today" after her amendment failed [00:25:17].

## 3. TOPIC FLAGS

### Economic Development
The meeting extensively discussed Prosper Portland's economic development programs, particularly the Inclusive Business Resource Network (IBRN) serving minority-owned businesses. Loretta Smith (District 1) successfully restored $197,000 for IBRN, emphasizing support for Filipino, African American, Latino, and Asian chambers of commerce [00:45:01]. The program provides culturally specific technical assistance and capital access for women, people of color, and non-English speaking entrepreneurs.

Discussion also covered the Reimagine Oregon program supporting cannabis-impacted communities, with $1.9 million remaining after Jamie Dunphy's (District 1) failed attempt to redirect $130,000 for youth services. Cannabis industry representatives reportedly lack voice in fund oversight despite paying the underlying taxes [00:55:41].

### Real Estate & Housing  
Limited direct housing discussion, though the immigrant affairs liaison position debate touched on housing security for immigrant communities. Candace Avalos (District 1) noted that "Su Casa Supermercado was down 40% last year because people are afraid to leave their homes" [00:11:29], indicating housing-related impacts of immigration enforcement.

### Climate & Sustainability Policy
PCF dominated climate discussions, with approximately $3.8 million in disputed allocations. Candice Avalos (District 1) argued the Mayor's diversions to sidewalk cleaning violated the "hard-fought community campaign rooted in climate justice" and warned of legal risks [01:13:16]. She emphasized that "East Portland has an average life expectancy 10 years less than the rest of the city as a result of environmental injustice."

Steve Novick (District 3) proposed complex fund swapping to preserve "intellectual integrity of PCF" while maintaining services, but acknowledged the stormwater infrastructure BES was using PCF for was work "BES should be doing anyway" [01:17:44]. Multiple councilors expressed concern about treating PCF as a "general fund piggy bank."

### System Development Charges (SDCs) & Permitting Fees
Tree permitting emerged as a critical issue with multiple restoration amendments proposed. Tiffany Koyama Lane (District 3) detailed how tree permit wait times increased from 2 weeks to 8 weeks due to staffing cuts, leading to unpermitted removals [01:51:39]. The program dropped from roughly 40 positions to 26, with the Mayor proposing another 14-position cut.

Debate centered on using PCF contingency funds versus protecting future tree planting capacity. Staff indicated the SP 34 program has $105 million allocated with $10 million contingency over 3-5 years, suggesting sustainable draw of $2-3 million annually [02:02:59].

### City Budget & Finance  
Jonas Biery (CFO / Deputy City Administrator for Budget & Finance) warned that cannabis revenue is "a declining revenue stream" when questioned about additional draws from that fund [00:49:24]. The overall budget showed continued pressure on core services, with multiple failed attempts to restore positions and programs.

Transportation Operating Fund discussions revealed only $750,000 available for emergencies like snow response, while historical major events cost around $10 million [00:32:01]. This influenced opposition to Jamie Dunphy's (District 1) infrastructure amendment.

### Infrastructure & Transportation
PBOT Director Millicent Williams detailed infrastructure challenges, noting "disproportionate impacts on District 1" for pothole and streetlight issues [00:29:47]. The discussion included innovative approaches like aluminum conductors for streetlights to deter copper wire theft, though specific funding wasn't secured.

The Transportation User Fee (TUF) and Street Damage Restoration Fee were mentioned as providing future opportunities for infrastructure investment without drawing from emergency contingency funds.
